A whole world
of Whimsy.

Many campaigns. Many visual styles. One connected experience.

Whimsy brings consulting, media, marketing, and website development together. I designed and built its public website to give that breadth a home: a substantial collection of campaign stories, local businesses, community events, creative services, and software.

The challenge was to make every project feel like itself while keeping the whole site easy to explore. Photography, illustration, promotional graphics, and distinct page treatments do much of the storytelling.

Art direction across the site

A different mood.
A familiar way to explore.

A holiday celebration, a farm, a deli, and an awareness campaign each deserve their own visual voice. Colors, decorative details, image arrangements, and pacing change with the subject. Shared navigation, readable headings, and a consistent path back to the work collection keep those individual styles connected.

Campaigns with depth

The whole story,
from invitation to event.

One campaign can contain an event poster, a series of partner advertisements, product photography, live coverage, and a recap. The site brings these pieces together so visitors can follow how the work develops, with room for the individual businesses and people involved.

A second kind of gathering

Designed around the people taking part.

Back to School Bash connects practical information about a quieter, family-focused event with individual promotions for photos, library resources, activities, and giveaways. Event photographs then show those plans becoming a shared experience.

Alongside seasonal events, the collection includes student art, local food, nonprofit work, business launches, and brand identities. Giving each story a dedicated page makes that range understandable without compressing everything into one gallery.

A substantial content collection

Dozens of pages.
Clear paths between them.

The live site links to 33 content pages: 20 project case studies, eight service pages, and five core destinations—home, about, work, services, and contact. The work gallery organizes its projects into Businesses, Community Events, Restaurants, Seasonal Events, and Software. The Warden case study also links to its live monitoring dashboard.

Category filters help visitors narrow the collection. Project pages connect the creative work to its context, while service descriptions and consultation links give prospective clients a clear next step.

From creative work to useful services

Let the images lead.
Keep the next step clear.

The visual approach carries into service pages. The advertising page uses a coordinated Sisters Smoothies series to show what a campaign can look like, alongside its scope, process, and deliverables. On smaller screens, the homepage rearranges its collage, navigation, and content into a vertical reading experience.
